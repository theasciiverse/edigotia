**ONE CITY. ONE LORD ABOVE YOU. FIVE KINGDOMS AROUND YOU. A CROWN WAITING FOR SOMEONE BOLD ENOUGH.**

First, conjure a world. Choose its size, its climate, its rainfall and its mountains, and watch the kingdoms rise to claim it: humans and elves, dwarves and orcs, goblins and ogres, trolls, halflings, gnomes, minotaurs, rakshasa and naga, each with a capital, duchy seats and towns strung along their roads. Then pick your people, choose a patch of wild land at the edge of your kingdom, name your town, and begin with a hundred peasants and a keep.

Every tile around your walls is real land. Here the soil is good for wheat; there the forest hides deer and boar; up in the hills lie tin, copper and iron. Lay out farms and pastures, sink mines, raise hovels and houses, draw in merchants and burghers, and set each class's taxes without souring their mood. Build the smeltery, the tailor and the mint, and turn ore into iron and fur into coats.

And you are not alone. The world beyond your walls is alive. The other cities eat and grow and go hungry, keep garrisons and pay them, and send out settlers to found new towns. Your Minister of Trade strikes standing deals with them: a hundred food a day from the farming town down the road, iron ore from the dwarven mine in the hills, your surplus coats to the city that has none. Every price is spoken plainly, the caravan's overhead counted in, and nobody haggles.

You are a baron, sworn to a duke, who is sworn to the crown. Each month your taxes are due. Now and then your liege sends a demand: gold for the treasury, food for the granaries, soldiers for a war, an end to your trade with a city they distrust. Comply, plead for patience, or refuse, and watch your favor rise and fall. Ask for a loan in a lean winter. Lords of every race keep their own ways: orcs expect their cities to fight each other, elves will not stand for it, gnomes tax hard and lend gladly.

Walk into the court of any city you know and speak with its mayor, its duke or its monarch. Buy a trade pact, a peace pact or an alliance at the price they name. Send a gift. Demand tribute, if you think they fear you. Or declare war.

Draft your peasants into militia, spearmen and swordsmen, your merchants into archers, your burghers into commanders who make every hundred soldiers fight harder. March them across the world map, beat a garrison in the field, and sit outside the walls until the city falls and swears itself to you. Meanwhile the kingdoms quarrel over old grudges and shared borders, declare their own wars, and trade cities back and forth, and when your king goes to war, his enemies are yours. Raiders come out of the hills for your stores. An orcish warlord sends envoys: pay, or he comes for it.

There is a crown at the end of it all:

- **Take it by the sword.** March on your kingdom's capital and hold its throne.
- **Win it by favor.** Rise in the crown's esteem until the monarch names you a duke, with towns of your own to pay you tribute, and be the duke the court trusts when the old monarch dies.
- **Found a kingdom of your own.** If your people have no kingdom in this world, rule four cities besides your own and proclaim one.

Lose your keep to a siege, or let your people drift away, and your reign is over.

- **A living world** of five kingdoms and hundreds of cities that grow, trade, quarrel, go to war and found new towns, every game its own
- **Trade** by standing deals in food, wood, luxuries and ore, with plain prices and caravan overheads
- **Diplomacy** in the courts of every city you know: treaties, gifts, demands, war and peace
- **Fealty** to a liege whose taxes, demands and favor follow the customs of their race
- **Armies and sieges** across the world map, five kinds of soldier, and cities that swear to you when they fall
- **The city at its heart**: land that matters, three classes of citizen, workshops and ministers' reports

## Controls

- On the first screen: **G** generates a world, **N** founds a city in
  it, **L** loads a saved city, **H** opens the help
- **1** to **5**: the Game, Ministers, Buildings, World and Help menus
- **A**: build an area (pick its kind, place it with the movement keys
  and **Enter**); **C**, **O** and **D**: close, reopen and destroy one
- **.** and **>**: let a day or a week pass (news that cannot wait stops
  the week)
- **2** then **7**: the Minister of Trade, **B** to buy, **S** to sell
- **4** then **2**: Diplomacy; **4** then **3**: the Military; **4**
  then **4**: the Realm; **4** then **1**: the world map
- **1** then **1**: save and quit; **Load city** on the first screen
  picks it up again

## History

In the spring of 2014, Whales, the creator of the roguelike Cataclysm, took
a break from his zombies and started something he had always wanted to
make: a strategy game. He called it "Civilization, but you only control one
city... kind of", and he wrote its subtitle on the start screen: a game of
building, trade, diplomacy and war.

He built the city first, in the open on GitHub, and it grew rich. On his
blog he told the story of the game he was aiming for: a gnome named Pilute
who borrows gold from his duke, buys axes from the dwarves, sells them to
the orcs with orc guards on the caravan, marries the duke's daughter, and
then faces a warlord at his gate demanding weapons or war. He sketched the
screens to come, a military roster and a court where a king makes demands of
his vassal, and wrote down what every race's lords expect of their cities.

By May 2015 the city was nearly complete, and he wrote that the one big
thing still missing was actual interaction with other cities. That June he
set the game aside, nearly nine hundred commits in, and the kingdoms around
the player's walls stood still, waiting.

They are moving now. Built from his blog, his notes and his sketches, the
trade, the courts, the armies and the crown he planned are here at last,
and Pilute's story can finally be played.
